# Ryan Case File Synthesis

## Scope

This document synthesizes the reconstructed record of Ryan, the subject of this file, across his interpersonal, therapeutic, family, medication, digital, and suicide timeline from 2023 through February 2025. The record was reconstructed by Garrett, Ryan's cousin, from prior case-file/addendum analysis and later recovered-device evidence: text messages, WhatsApp patterns, photographs, browser history, Amazon activity, and witness recollections.

This is not a clinical diagnosis, legal conclusion, or finding of a single cause of death. The record supports a multi-factor process: mood deterioration, social withdrawal, romantic ambiguity, self-esteem regulation through attention from women, therapeutic framing, family/developmental context, selective self-presentation, digital deletion, and eventual suicide preparation. None of these should be treated as sufficient by itself.

## Evidence Key

**Established fact** means the item is described as directly present in the recovered record, such as a dated message, photograph, Amazon record, browser history entry, or documented device artifact.

**Corroborated observation** means multiple independent records or witnesses point in the same direction, even if no single item proves the whole interpretation.

**Plausible inference** means the interpretation fits the chronology and evidence but remains uncertain.

**Unknown** means the record does not establish the answer. Unknowns should not be filled in with blame or certainty.

## Executive Synthesis

The strongest reconstruction is that Ryan's final months were not defined by one event or one person. The evidence instead shows a process that began well before the immediate suicide window.

By spring 2024, Ryan was already in a clinically complicated period involving mood swings, Lexapro/escitalopram use or possible resumption, psychiatric contact, severe internal activation, and later a dramatic upswing that both Ryan and Briana discussed using the language of possible hypomania. By summer 2024, Ryan was highly socially active and unusually responsive to attention from women. He explicitly recognized that female attention, confidence, self-esteem, self-worth, and mood were tightly linked for him.

Ryan's tendency toward romantic absorption appears to predate the final year. In a relationship with Lauren around 2015, Ryan, who was essentially atheist or nonreligious, reportedly dated a strongly Christian and sexually conservative woman for years. They reportedly did not have intercourse, although Ryan said they did "everything but that." Ryan visited her at her parents' home on weekends, slept on the downstairs couch, and did not have Lauren sleeping over. He complained about her thinking as illogical for years but, when Garrett asked why he stayed, answered in substance: "Cuz I like her?" In the later Cara relationship, Ryan reportedly dropped much of his own friend network, spent time primarily with Cara's friends, and joined her family's summer vacations.

There is also an older mood/self-focus pattern. Garrett and Heather affectionately called Ryan "Eeyore," especially around 2021, when he was remembered as moody and prone to "woe is me" speeches about relationships, erectile dysfunction, work, and other problems. During a New Hampshire cabin trip, John reportedly drove Ryan from Pittsburgh to New Hampshire because John did not want to fly and rent a car. John later said Ryan did not offer to take a driving shift, talked extensively about himself and his problems, and did not offer gas money. At the cabin, Ryan reportedly spent much of the time disengaged, taking naps, including falling asleep during a river float and initially declining to join group games.

During the 2023-2024 period, Ryan increasingly interpreted difficult interpersonal interactions through psychological categories: narcissism, borderline personality disorder, manipulation, control, toxicity, emotional unavailability, and similar labels. That tendency appears to predate Nienke. It appears in relation to Cara in 2023, Garrett in 2023, Sarah, Sasha, Savannah, Mallory, and possibly broader dating categories such as nurses or dancers. Nienke is the conspicuous exception. Despite being partnered, emotionally ambiguous, physically affectionate, and sometimes asking for significant practical help, she does not appear in the recovered record as someone Ryan pathologized. Instead, he describes her as sweet, special, affectionate, and possibly romantically interested.

The Nienke relationship was behaviorally intense regardless of its label. The known record includes high-volume WhatsApp communication, repeated one-on-one outings, shared activities, transportation and logistical support, dancing, dinners, apartment visits, a marathon trip, family introduction, affectionate messages, and physical-affection reports made by Ryan to friends. The relationship was also triangular: Maarten was present in the background and sometimes physically present, while Ryan privately evaluated Maarten, contrasted himself with him, and described himself as "waiting in the wings." Friends often reinforced the view that Nienke was romantically interested in Ryan. The recovered conversations do not show comparable concern for Maarten's perspective.

The October 2024 marathon weekend was emotionally important but should not be overstated as a single cause. Ryan had reason to view it as a possible test of the relationship: he drove Nienke, stayed with her, supported her race, danced with her by a lake, introduced her to his parents, and received a warm birthday card afterward. But the card also described him as a friend, and Ryan later reported "no canoodling." Soon afterward he sought online dating advice and dated other women. Yet the recovered photo record remains dominated by Nienke, not by the women he said he was dating.

By late October 2024, Ryan's depressive downturn is documented independently of Nienke. On October 27, he told Briana he had been down all weekend, had less energy for hobbies, exercise, and routines, and was sleeping a lot. Briana recognized this as similar to seasonal depression after his birthday, though later than the prior year. On October 28, Ryan told Olivia his mood, motivation, and energy were sinking, that he was considering leaving the dance performance team, sleeping more, tired all the time, inconsistent with lifting, talking to his therapist, and using a light box. This strongly supports an October clinical inflection point rather than a January onset.

From November through January, the Nienke relationship and Ryan's broader functioning moved in opposite directions. Nienke's life expanded outward through parents, NYC, the Southwest, Europe, and Hawaii. Ryan's world contracted: less dancing, less gym, more sleep, fewer routines, lower energy, more isolation, declining invitations, and minimizing concern from friends. Nienke's communication dropped sharply by message count, but by January the initiation pattern reversed completely: Ryan initiated zero active conversation days while Nienke initiated all of them. She did not disappear when he stopped being useful for rides.

The late January suicide window is the most significant concrete transition. Around January 25-26, Nienke confronted Ryan about ignoring her and asked whether he still wanted to be friends. Ryan said he was feeling exceptionally down, needed time alone, and that she had done nothing wrong. Around the same period, the Amazon history shows purchase of the rope later associated with his death. That timing is important, but it does not establish that Nienke caused the purchase or the suicide. It more defensibly suggests that by late January, the depressive withdrawal may have moved into concrete preparation.

By February, several people were trying to reach Ryan: Nienke, Olivia, Brad, Aaron, John, Charlie, Joe, Lucas, and others. Brad and John explicitly questioned whether Ryan was really okay. Joe and Lucas, Ryan's neighbors, reportedly observed that from December through February Ryan did not seem to be taking good care of himself, sometimes appeared not to be showering, and came over almost daily simply to hang out. They cooked extra food because they were concerned he might not be eating properly. At the same time, Ryan still engaged in ordinary future-oriented acts: paying toward the upcoming bowling competition season, calculating his bowling-fee share on his phone, and apparently having dinner plans for the following week. Those acts complicate the picture but do not negate the rope evidence. Ryan increasingly minimized his condition as tiredness or busyness. His browsing history in the final weeks included death prediction, clairvoyance/paranormal concepts, psychiatric terms, and quantum immortality, but those searches do not prove belief or intent by themselves. The final known text was reportedly to Drew around 9 PM on February 15, 2025.

The case file therefore supports a process, not a verdict: Ryan appears to have been increasingly depressed, increasingly withdrawn, and increasingly unreachable while surrounded by people who did care and were trying, but who did not have the whole picture.

## Chronological Reconstruction

### Circa 2015-Early 2023: Earlier Romantic Absorption and Mood/Self-Focus Pattern

**Established by witness report:** Around 2015, Ryan reportedly dated Lauren, a strongly Christian and sexually conservative woman, for years. Ryan was essentially atheist or nonreligious. The relationship reportedly did not include intercourse, although Ryan described it as involving "everything but that."

**Established by witness report:** Ryan visited Lauren at her parents' house on weekends and slept on the downstairs couch. Lauren reportedly did not sleep over at Ryan's place.

**Established by witness report:** Ryan complained for years that Lauren's thinking was illogical. When Garrett asked why he remained in the relationship, Ryan reportedly answered in substance: "Cuz I like her?"

**Established by witness report:** During the later Cara relationship, Ryan reportedly dropped much of his own friend network, spent time with Cara's friends, and attended Cara's family summer vacations.

**Corroborated observation:** These earlier relationships suggest a recurring pattern of partner-centered accommodation: Ryan could accept a girlfriend's religious, sexual, family, or social terms even while complaining about them or allowing his own independent friendships to recede.

**Plausible inference:** This pattern helps contextualize Nienke. Ryan's later investment in Nienke was not only a one-off crush on an unavailable woman. It fits a broader tendency to reorganize his social world around a woman he liked, tolerate relational constraints, and remain attached even when the arrangement was frustrating or mismatched.

**Established by witness report:** In 2021, Garrett and Heather affectionately referred to Ryan as "Eeyore" because he was remembered as moody and prone to "woe is me" rants or speeches about relationships, erectile dysfunction, his job, and other frustrations.

**Established by witness report:** During a New Hampshire cabin trip, John drove Ryan from Pittsburgh to New Hampshire. John reportedly said Ryan did not offer to take a driving shift, talked extensively about himself and his problems, and did not offer gas money. At the cabin, Ryan reportedly spent substantial time disengaged or napping, including falling asleep during a river float and initially declining to join group games.

**Corroborated observation:** This suggests that some elements visible in the final year--low mood, self-focused distress narration, disengagement, and napping during group activity--had earlier analogues. The New Hampshire material should not be retroactively treated as evidence of the 2024-2025 depressive episode, but it does show that Ryan's friends had seen earlier versions of him as moody, self-absorbed, or difficult to engage.

**Established by witness report:** In 2022, Ryan's Facebook updated to show he was in a relationship, which was news to Garrett despite their closeness. Garrett objected that Ryan tended to share bad news with him but not good news. Ryan did not apologize and instead countered that Garrett had not told him about Heather, even though Garrett had met Heather in January 2021 and Ryan had gone on vacation with Garrett and Heather to Puerto Rico by May 2021.

**Plausible inference:** The 2022 Facebook conflict supports two later themes: Ryan's tendency to let romantic developments reach close people indirectly or belatedly, and his tendency during conflict to answer a request for acknowledgment with a counter-accusation rather than repair.

### 2023: Earlier Interpersonal Pattern

**Established/corroborated:** By early 2023, after Cara ended the relationship, Ryan reportedly told Garrett that his therapist believed Cara might have borderline personality disorder or similar pathology. Garrett challenged that framing at the time because it seemed convenient that Cara's mental-health framing emerged after she broke up with Ryan.

**Corroborated observation:** By summer 2023, Ryan's language of manipulation and narcissism appeared to generalize beyond romantic relationships. Garrett and Heather independently noticed Ryan applying psychological labels to relatively ordinary conflict or joking interactions.

**Plausible inference:** This suggests that Ryan's later 2024 pathologizing was not merely a reaction to one bad dating experience. It may have been an increasingly available interpretive framework: conflict was often understood through the other person's disorder, manipulation, or pathology.

### Family/Home Context

**Established by witness report:** Ryan's family home is described as a chronically unfinished and dysfunctional environment. Specific examples include a rear patio removed decades earlier and never replaced, a sliding patio door opening onto a significant drop, carpet removed and left as plywood for decades, door trim never finished after remodeling began, and basic home functioning such as a working shower reportedly absent for years.

**Plausible inference:** This environment may have normalized chronic incompletion: visible problems could remain unresolved indefinitely. This is not evidence of a diagnosis for Ryan or his parents. It is relevant context for Ryan's later patterns of unfinished obligations, avoidance, deferred repair, and tolerated ambiguity.

**Unknown:** The record does not establish how Ryan internally experienced the house or whether he consciously linked it to his own functioning.

### December 2023: Vehicle, Boxes, and Self-Presentation

**Established/corroborated:** Ryan's 4Runner-to-RAV4 transition produced inconsistent or incomplete accounts to different audiences. Joe and Lucas reportedly understood that Ryan was paying off the car. Heather was told Ryan would "have to check" the rate. Ryan's mother said she and Charlie bought it for him, and the record includes Charlie asking Ryan to bring the title home in December 2024, which is consistent with the vehicle being paid off.

**Established by witness/recovered record:** Ryan later explained the dealership purchase by saying that when he went to the dealership that day, he had no intention of buying a car. Garrett's reconstruction is that this is not credible as a full explanation if Ryan arrived ready to trade in his car and with a new car in mind.

**Corroborated observation:** Ryan appears to have accepted money or valuable gifts from his parents while minimizing or obscuring that dependency to other people.

**Plausible inference:** Ryan may have been invested in appearing more financially independent and self-directed than he actually was. This does not require assuming an elaborate lie in every instance. It may reflect self-protective impression management: selective disclosure, omission, adjacent answers, and accounts shaped to preserve a preferred self-image.

**Boxes parallel:** Around the same broader period, Garrett had stored boxes at Ryan's place and later asked Ryan to mail them, offering to pay shipping. Ryan agreed but did not send them. Months later, Ryan told Briana that Garrett had asked him to mail the boxes, but reframed the situation as no longer being Garrett's "U-Haul storage." The missing accountability question in the recovered exchange is why Ryan had not mailed the boxes after agreeing to do so.

### March-May 2024: Therapy, Lexapro, Activation, and Labeling

**Established fact:** On March 28, Ryan reported mood swings.

**Established fact:** Around April 2-3, Ryan was distressed enough to request additional contact, seek psychiatric care, resume or consider resuming 2.5 mg Lexapro, leave work early, and describe feeling as though he were "jumping out of my skin."

**Established fact:** By late April, the direction appeared to reverse. Ryan described himself in markedly elevated terms, including "sunshine." Briana asked, "Are we a bit hypomanic still?" Ryan replied, "I think so." Ryan was tracking sleep and mood and discussing changes with his psychiatrist.

**Medication timing considerations:** Lexapro/escitalopram is an SSRI. Authoritative medication guidance states that patients may notice improvement in 1 to 4 weeks, while full benefit can take several weeks; it should be taken as directed and not stopped abruptly without medical guidance. The Lexapro label also includes warnings to monitor for clinical worsening or suicidal thoughts and includes activation of mania or hypomania as a known warning/precaution. These general facts do not prove what happened to Ryan. They only mean that actual dose, adherence, start/stop timing, adverse effects, and psychiatric follow-up matter. The recovered record does not yet establish those details.

**Unknown:** Whether Ryan took Lexapro consistently, at what dose, for how long, whether he stopped or restarted, and whether any medication effect contributed to spring activation, fall depression, or February suicide risk. Those remain open clinical questions.

### Spring 2024: Briana and the Therapeutic Relationship

**Established/corroborated:** Ryan had a high volume of texts with Briana, his therapist. The messages included more than scheduling. Ryan appears to have used her as a running clinical/interpersonal log: dating commentary, screenshots, emotional check-ins, symptom documentation, jokes, and interpretations of other people.

**Corroborated observation:** The relationship appears unusually informal and close from the surviving texts. That does not itself prove improper care. Some therapists allow structured between-session messaging. The relevant questions are boundary clarity, clinical purpose, what occurred in actual sessions, how risk was assessed, and whether Ryan's self-focused accountability was being explored.

**Established/corroborated:** Briana appears in Ryan's retellings as supplying or reinforcing interpretations of absent third parties: Mallory as controlling, possible BPD material, narcissistic tendencies, and other categories. Ryan also told Olivia that Briana strongly discouraged him from dating nurses and characterized nurses in broadly negative terms. Because this comes through Ryan's retelling, it should not be treated as a verbatim clinical statement unless the direct Briana text is found.

**Plausible inference:** Therapy may have given Ryan a language for identifying unhealthy dynamics, but may also have reinforced his tendency to locate the problem in other people rather than examine his own role in conflict, avoidance, and repair. This is a hypothesis about the recovered text pattern, not a professional conclusion about Briana.

**Notable absence:** In the surviving Briana texts reviewed, Nienke does not appear. This is notable because Ryan discussed Nienke extensively with friends and because Nienke occupied large portions of his photo and WhatsApp record. It remains unknown whether Ryan discussed Nienke in verbal sessions.

### May-July 2024: Attention, Mood, and Self-Worth

**Established fact:** Ryan became unusually socially active through dance, dating apps, fitness, events, and women's attention.

**Established fact:** Ryan explicitly told Olivia that he did not usually receive this much attention and that it felt like a "positive feedback loop." He also stated that the experience showed him how much self-confidence, self-esteem, self-worth, and mood were tied together.

**Corroborated observation:** Female attention appears to have been mood-regulating for Ryan. It boosted confidence, desirability, and social energy during what he framed as an unusually successful summer.

**Plausible inference:** This made Ryan vulnerable to mood changes when attention became ambiguous, inconsistent, or unavailable. The record does not support saying attention from women caused his later depression, but it supports the view that attention and self-worth were unusually intertwined for him.

Even if it remained formally platonic, the behavioral pattern resembled a significant emotional attachment: frequent contact, shared activities, physical affection, practical support, logistical planning, trips, meals, dancing, and family/social integration.

### September 2024: The Maarten Triangle

**Established fact:** Ryan knew Nienke had a boyfriend, Maarten, and spent time with both of them. Maarten's visit lasted weeks, not just a brief appearance.

**Established fact:** Ryan told Olivia that Nienke said she and Maarten were in an open relationship but that Ryan did not know what that meant.

**Established fact:** Ryan told Mackenzie he was "keeping this platonic" but also described Nienke and Maarten fighting, Maarten being a "dick" to her, Maarten's jail/arrest history, and Maarten refusing to dance.

**Established fact:** Ryan told Mackenzie he was "waiting in the wings" to hear about the fallout, listing possibilities that he would be excommunicated or that they would break up. He also interpreted Nienke asking about sensual bachata as meaningful and said he was "playing it cool."

**Established/corroborated:** Ryan told another friend that observers would think he and Nienke were "fuckin every chance we get," and described repeated physical contact: holding, wanting to be held, her head on his shoulder, and her wanting him to touch/brace her. This does not prove Nienke wanted a relationship, but it strongly supports that Ryan's confusion was not based only on texts.

**Corroborated observation:** Ryan was not merely attracted from a distance. He was in a triangular emotional structure where Maarten was present, Ryan evaluated Maarten's inadequacies, Ryan saw himself as providing what Maarten did not, and Ryan privately left open the possibility that Nienke's relationship would fail.

**Notable absence:** In the recovered friend discussions, people evaluate Nienke's likely feelings, Ryan's chances, Maarten's shortcomings, and whether Nienke might make a move. There is little to no recovered discussion of how Maarten might reasonably experience Ryan's role, whether Maarten consented emotionally to this dynamic, or whether Ryan's "waiting in the wings" position itself created pressure.

### September 2024: Mallory Contrast

**Established fact:** Ryan described Mallory as being in a five-year relationship while wanting to ensure she had "something in place" before ending it. Ryan challenged this and asked whether she would need time and space after a five-year relationship.

**Established fact:** Ryan reported that his therapist said Mallory was trying to control him/the situation when convenient for her. Mackenzie agreed and suggested Ryan could become a scapegoat.

**Corroborated observation:** Ryan treated Mallory's ambiguity as a warning sign but treated Nienke's ambiguity as exciting and meaningful. With Mallory, a partnered woman exploring a possible successor was framed as controlling. With Nienke, Ryan himself occupied something like a possible successor position while describing himself as "waiting in the wings."

**Plausible inference:** This is one of the clearest examples of Ryan being better at detecting problematic dynamics when he was outside them than when he was emotionally invested in them.

### October 2024: Marathon Weekend and Aftermath

**Established/corroborated:** Ryan drove Nienke to her marathon while Maarten remained in Pittsburgh. Ryan likely saw this as an opportunity for one-on-one time away from Maarten. He and Nienke shared lodging or at minimum managed lodging together; the direct WhatsApp record appears more logistical than Ryan's more suggestive retelling to Olivia.

**Established/corroborated:** Ryan supported her race, danced with her by a lake/dock, introduced her to his parents, and received a handwritten birthday card on or around October 7. The card thanked him for coming, called him her best spectator, thanked him overall for being her friend, and said he made her time in Pittsburgh "1000x better."

**Established fact:** Ryan sent the card to Mackenzie. That matters because Mackenzie was one of the people with whom he had been analyzing whether Nienke liked him. His contemporaneous act of sharing the card suggests he experienced it positively, not simply as a rejection.

**Established fact:** Ryan reported "no canoodling" after the trip and soon asked Olivia for online-dating advice.

**Plausible inference:** The marathon weekend may have functioned as a reality test. It provided many conditions Ryan associated with romantic possibility: travel, support, physical closeness, family introduction, and time away from Maarten. The result did not move into romance. But because the card was warm and the friendship continued, the ambiguity did not end cleanly.

### October-December 2024: Depression Emerges While Nienke Remains Present

**Established fact:** On October 20, Ryan and Nienke went to Kinzua Bridge and he appeared to be having a good time in photos.

**Established fact:** On October 26, Ryan and Nienke went to Trace Brewing and took a selfie.

**Established fact:** On October 27, Ryan texted Briana that he had been feeling down all weekend, that activities required more energy, that hobbies/exercise/routines were harder, and that he was sleeping a lot. Briana identified the pattern as seasonal depression, later than the prior year, and suggested psychiatrist contact; she said half a dose would not hurt but wanted the psychiatrist's view.

**Established fact:** On October 28, Ryan told Olivia his mood, motivation, and energy were sinking, that he might leave the dance performance team, was sleeping more, tired all the time, talking to his therapist, had bought a light box, and had become inconsistent with lifting.

**Corroborated observation:** The depressive downturn was global, not simply Nienke-specific. It involved mood, sleep, energy, exercise, dance, hobbies, routines, and work.

**Established/corroborated:** Ryan dated Beth and other women during this period. He told Nienke about Beth kissing him and described the physical details in unusual detail. Nienke appeared to respond as a supportive friend rather than with jealousy. By Christmas, Ryan told Rob, his lifelong friend, about Sreyashi Mukherjee, an Indian woman he was seeing, and did not mention Nienke. Sreyashi existed and apparently faded by early January 2025, but left little surviving record.

**Corroborated observation:** Ryan presented different versions of his romantic life to different audiences. To Rob, the dated woman was the salient story. In the recovered photo record, Nienke is the dominant female presence. To Olivia and Mackenzie, Nienke is the ambiguous romantic focus. To Nienke, Ryan presents his dating life, possibly in a way that sought reaction, though intent is unknown.

**Collateral account from Olivia:** Olivia later described Ryan as "completely obsessed" with Nienke and said the relationship looked like dating at baseline, including solo trips such as Ryan driving Nienke to her marathon in Seneca/New York. Olivia interpreted the marathon trip as an excuse for Ryan to spend time with Nienke away from Maarten and said she was convinced Ryan and Nienke had been hooking up. This hooking-up belief is Olivia's interpretation; the recovered WhatsApp record does not independently confirm it.

**Collateral account from Olivia:** Olivia also reported that some women in Ryan's salsa/dance classes were interested in or dating Ryan, but that Nienke acted protective and seemed to drive them away. Olivia described several women, including Nienke, as arguing with Ryan about his intentions and interests. Olivia met two of the women and found them unpleasant. This account is relevant because it portrays Nienke not simply as a passive friend, but as someone Olivia perceived as protective or possibly territorial around Ryan.

### November-December 2024: Communication Decline and Travel

**Established/corroborated:** Nienke's life became more expansive: parents visiting, NYC, Southwest travel, Europe, and later Hawaii. She sent Ryan photos and updates from travel; he sometimes did not engage.

**Established/corroborated:** The WhatsApp record also suggests a possible late-October-through-holiday asymmetry. Nienke appears to have left town at least at times without saying goodbye, some later contact appears oriented around practical needs such as pickup/rides, and Ryan's Christmas and New Year's messages were not promptly answered or reciprocated.

**Established/corroborated:** Ryan's functioning contracted. He communicated fatigue, brain fog, less dancing, trouble returning to the gym, work draining him, bowling "keeping me grounded," and less optimism around dating.

**Plausible inference:** The message decline likely reflects two overlapping processes: Nienke's physical/lifestyle unavailability and Ryan's progressive depressive withdrawal. But the unanswered holiday messages and practical-contact pattern could also have made the relationship feel asymmetrical to Ryan: he may have experienced himself as available for support, logistics, attention, and emotional labor while receiving less ordinary continuity or acknowledgment in return. Travel alone cannot explain the January pattern because when Nienke returned and tried to reconnect, Ryan still did not reengage.

### January 2025: Deletion, Withdrawal, and Reversal

**Established by recovered digital record:** On January 10, Ryan appears to have deleted all messages. The exact technical scope, target app(s), and motive are unknown.

**Established/corroborated:** On January 10, Ryan also declined Olivia's invitation, saying he had been feeling rundown and needed to recuperate, not sure whether he was sick.

**Established/corroborated:** Early January messages to Brad show Ryan saying he was being a hermit and that his condition was both physical and mental. This suggests he was not yet concealing everything, but was already describing withdrawal.

**Established/corroborated:** Nienke asked for an airport ride on January 9. Ryan did not provide it. Importantly, she continued trying to see him afterward, which weighs against the strongest version of the theory that she was simply using him for transportation.

**Established/corroborated:** By late January, Ryan had stopped initiating with Nienke. Nienke called, asked if he was alive, asked if he still wanted to be friends, told him his ignoring her was hurtful, and asked him to communicate clearly. Ryan said he had been feeling exceptionally down, needed time alone, and that she had done nothing wrong. Nienke warned that further isolation would not help.

**Established/corroborated:** When Nienke came to Ryan's apartment, Ryan reportedly did not let her in and appeared to pretend he was sleeping. This occurred despite evidence that Ryan could still go to Joe and Lucas's house and participate at least intermittently in bowling-related plans or activity.

**Corroborated observation:** This makes Ryan's late-stage withdrawal more selective than absolute. He was not simply unreachable in every context. He appears to have avoided higher-stakes, emotionally charged contact with Nienke while still sometimes accepting lower-demand proximity with neighbors or structured social routines such as bowling.

**Established/corroborated:** Around the same January 26 period, Amazon history shows Ryan purchased the rope later associated with his death. The rope was a half-inch utility rope, not a specialized climbing rope, and Ryan was not known to be a climber. It was reportedly the only item he ordered from Amazon in 2025.

**Plausible inference:** January 26 is a major convergence date: social confrontation, explicit depressive disclosure, desire to isolate, and possible suicide preparation occur in the same narrow window. The evidence supports marking this as a transition into higher danger. It does not establish that Nienke's confrontation caused the purchase or death.

### Late January-February 2025: Friends, Neighbors, and Coworkers Attempt Contact

**Established/corroborated:** Olivia invited Ryan to football, dinner, ramen, and other activities. ## Integrated Thematic Findings

### 1. Ryan's Relationship With Briana

The therapist relationship appears unusually close and informal in the recovered text record. That does not automatically make it improper. Some clinicians allow between-session messaging for scheduling, symptom tracking, homework, brief support, or documentation before sessions. What makes Ryan's relationship with Briana notable is the breadth of material: dating, screenshots, jokes, emotional interpretation, third-party labels, medication questions, and ongoing symptom reports.

The therapeutic relationship may have been both a support and an interpretive amplifier. Ryan appears to have brought interpersonal uncertainty to Briana, and at least in his retellings, Briana sometimes supplied language that framed the other person as controlling, narcissistic, borderline, manipulative, toxic, or otherwise psychologically suspect. In the boxes example, Briana appears to validate the goal of getting Garrett's property out of Ryan's place, but the recovered text does not show direct challenge of Ryan's own failure to mail the boxes after agreeing to do so.

The strongest fair formulation is this: Briana may have helped Ryan identify unhealthy patterns in other people, but the recovered record raises the question of whether therapy sufficiently challenged Ryan's own patterns of omission, avoidance, self-protective narration, and lack of repair. That remains a question, not a conclusion, because the content of in-person sessions is mostly unknown.

The absence of Nienke in surviving Briana texts is also significant. Ryan discussed Sarah and other women extensively with Briana or through Briana's interpretations. Yet Nienke, who occupied an enormous portion of Ryan's actual life and documentation, does not appear in the surviving therapist texts reviewed. That does not prove Briana did not know about her. It does suggest that the written therapist record does not connect Ryan's late-October depressive shift to Nienke.

### 2. Audience-Specific Self-Presentation

Ryan repeatedly appears to present different slices of reality to different audiences.

With Rob at Christmas, he discussed Sreyashi Mukherjee, an Indian woman he was seeing, and apparently did not mention Nienke. That is not necessarily false; Sreyashi existed. But it omits the person dominating Ryan's lived social record.

With Olivia, Ryan framed the marathon lodging as Nienke booking a hotel and asking him to stay with her. The direct WhatsApp record appears more logistical and less provocative, including Nienke asking about two rooms if possible. Ryan's version to Olivia emphasized romantic suggestiveness.

With Nienke, Ryan described kissing and dating other women in unusual detail. That may have been ordinary friend talk, but in context it may also have functioned as a way of signaling desirability or testing for jealousy.

With neighbors or friends, Ryan appears to have implied he was paying off the RAV4. With Heather, he suggested he would have to check the rate. With family, the record indicates his parents bought or substantially funded it and that the title was expected home. These versions preserve Ryan's image of independence more than they clarify the truth.

With Briana, Ryan acknowledged Garrett had asked him to mail the boxes, yet he later framed the issue as no longer being Garrett's storage. This shows how an account can include a key fact but still organize the story around Ryan as the imposed-upon party.

This pattern is best described as selective self-presentation rather than blanket lying. Some cases may involve direct falsehood; others may involve omission, emphasis, minimizing dependency, answering adjacent questions, or telling technically compatible but emotionally misleading versions.

### 3. Women, Attention, and Self-Esteem

Ryan's own words make this theme unusually strong. He recognized that women's attention affected his self-confidence, self-esteem, self-worth, and mood. Summer 2024 provided him with an unusual level of romantic/sexual/social reinforcement: dance attention, dates, numbers, physical fitness, social confidence, and a sense of momentum.

That makes Nienke's timing important. She entered the record during a period when Ryan was primed to experience female attention as self-expanding and mood-lifting. Her attention was not ordinary or minimal. It was frequent, affectionate, embodied, and behaviorally intimate.

The risk is that attention which initially regulated self-worth can destabilize self-worth when it becomes ambiguous or inconsistent. Ryan did not merely want dates in general; he appears to have wanted to understand whether Nienke wanted him specifically. Later dating did not erase that attachment. The photo archive suggests Beth and Sreyashi Mukherjee were less central to Ryan's memory-building than Nienke, even if they were more conventionally dateable.

### 4. Nienke as the Exception

One of the most important comparative findings is that Ryan pathologized many people but not Nienke.

Cara was associated with possible borderline pathology. Sarah with narcissism/manipulation and boundary problems. Sasha with narcissism. Savannah with manipulation and "Jekyll and Hyde" dynamics. Mallory with control, narcissistic traits, contingency planning, and scapegoating. Nurses and dancers were described through broad negative stereotypes or warnings.

Nienke, however, does not appear to receive that treatment in the recovered record. This is striking because, viewed structurally, she was exactly the kind of person Ryan might otherwise have pathologized: partnered, ambiguous, physically affectionate, emotionally confiding, accepting practical support, and maintaining a relationship that did not resolve into clear romance.

The plausible inference is that Ryan's attachment to Nienke protected her from the interpretive machinery he applied to others. He saw Mallory's ambiguity as controlling but Nienke's ambiguity as possibility. He saw Maarten's deficits clearly but had less interest in the ethical discomfort of his own "waiting in the wings" position.

Olivia's retrospective account strengthens the impression that Ryan remained protective of Nienke as an exception. In January, when Olivia told Ryan she did not like Nienke and found the relationship suspicious, Ryan reportedly remained defensive of the relationship rather than adopting the same suspicious/pathologizing stance he applied elsewhere.

### 5. The Maarten Blind Spot

Maarten was part of the relationship field, not a distant abstraction. Ryan met him, spent time with him, assessed him, heard about conflict between him and Nienke, knew about the open-relationship framing, and measured himself against Maarten's shortcomings.

Friends reinforced Ryan's romantic reading. Olivia suggested Nienke probably wanted him or "ached" for him. Mackenzie joked that Nienke and Maarten probably argued about Ryan. Ryan accepted those interpretations. The focus of the conversations was Ryan's opportunity, Nienke's possible interest, and Maarten's deficiencies.

What is largely absent is Maarten's perspective. There is little evidence that anyone asked whether Maarten might reasonably feel threatened, whether he understood Ryan's role the same way Nienke did, whether the "open" arrangement included this kind of emotional intimacy, or whether Ryan himself was becoming part of the couple's conflict.

This does not make Ryan malicious. It does show a moral and relational blind spot: the triangle was analyzed primarily from Ryan's vantage point.

Olivia later stated that she did not think it helped that many of Ryan's friends encouraged the Nienke possibility, telling Ryan that he and Nienke would be perfect together or looked picture-perfect. This matters because the romantic interpretation was not simply Ryan's private fantasy; it was socially reinforced.

Olivia also speculated that Ryan may have been "waiting out" for Nienke, that some kind of ultimatum may have occurred, and that it did not go Ryan's way. This remains speculation. No recovered record currently establishes that an ultimatum occurred.

### 6. Chauffeur/Caretaker Dynamic

The "chauffeur" dynamic is real, but the strongest exploitation version is not supported.

Ryan repeatedly provided transportation and logistical support: IKEA, errands, dance rides, chair transport, walking Nienke home, marathon travel, lodging research, route planning, airport pickup offers, and general practical support. Some of this was requested by Nienke; much of it was volunteered by Ryan. Nienke often phrased requests with outs and alternatives, and she expressed gratitude.

The pattern is better understood as asymmetric caretaking. Ryan seemed to use usefulness as a route to intimacy. Being the person who helped, drove, planned, supported, and made things easier appears connected to his self-concept. Nienke may have experienced this as help from a very close friend. Ryan may have experienced it as partner-like investment.

The late-October-through-holiday pattern adds a second asymmetry. After the intense September/early-October period, Nienke's travel and reduced responsiveness may have left Ryan feeling demoted from daily companion to occasional practical support. The WhatsApp record reportedly includes episodes where she left town without saying goodbye, later reached out around practical pickup needs, and did not promptly respond to Ryan's Christmas or New Year's messages. This does not prove exploitation, but it is relevant to how the relationship may have felt from Ryan's side.

January tests the exploitation hypothesis. When Ryan stopped providing rides and stopped engaging, Nienke continued reaching out. She called, asked whether he was alive, asked whether he wanted to remain friends, offered coffee/walks/events, and eventually offered to come to his apartment so he did not need to go out. This strongly suggests she cared about Ryan beyond his usefulness.

But the apartment episode also changes the meaning of "withdrawal." Ryan reportedly did not let Nienke in and appeared to pretend he was sleeping when she came to him. Yet he still went to Joe and Lucas's house and remained intermittently connected to bowling. That contrast suggests not simple physical isolation, but selective avoidance: Nienke may have represented emotional accountability, shame, romantic disappointment, or a relationship conversation Ryan could not tolerate, while Joe and Lucas offered passive, low-demand companionship.

### 7. Digital Deletion and Absence of Apology

The January 10 deletion matters because it occurs inside the broader transition from depressive symptoms into social withdrawal. If Ryan deleted all messages on or around that date, the act may indicate privacy management, shame, emotional reset, concealment, or ordinary digital cleanup. The motive is unknown.

The deletion also creates an evidentiary problem: absence after January 10 may partly reflect destruction of records rather than absence of events.

Separately, the recovered record reportedly contains no meaningful apology language or apology document from Ryan. That absence fits the broader pattern of avoidance rather than repair, but it cannot prove that Ryan never apologized verbally or in deleted messages. It should be recorded as a negative finding: no recovered apology evidence, not proof of no apology.

### 8. Historical Relationship Withdrawal

Ryan was reportedly known to vanish when he got a new girlfriend. People might not hear from him for months and then see a relationship-status update. This history matters because it shows social narrowing around romance was not unique to Nienke or late 2024.

The Lauren and Cara histories sharpen that pattern. With Lauren, Ryan appears to have accommodated a relationship structure shaped by her religion, sexuality, and family home, even while criticizing her thinking. With Cara, Ryan reportedly shifted heavily into Cara's social and family world, including her friends and family vacations. These examples suggest not merely withdrawal from friends, but a broader tendency toward relationship-centered social migration: Ryan's world could reorganize around the woman he was attached to.

The 2022 Facebook relationship-update conflict is a smaller but useful example. Garrett learned Ryan was in a relationship through Facebook rather than directly from Ryan, despite their closeness. When Garrett objected that Ryan tended to share distress and bad news but not good news, Ryan reportedly countered with a comparison to Garrett's relationship with Heather rather than apologizing. This fits the later pattern of indirect disclosure, defensive counter-comparison, and limited repair language.

However, January-February 2025 looks different. The withdrawal was not organized around a new relationship. It was global: Olivia, Brad, Aaron, John, Charlie, Nienke, Joe, Lucas, and others were all trying to reach him, invite him out, or observe a decline in daily self-care. Ryan declined or minimized. That distinction is important. His familiar "new girlfriend disappearance" may show a baseline pattern of absorption, but the final withdrawal appears depressive and generalized.

### 9. Post-Death Nienke Material

Nienke reportedly organized a Celebration of Life at the dance studio soon after Ryan's death and obtained parental approval. She apparently did not make substantial efforts to include Ryan's extended older friend network. Some long-standing friends who did not know her perceived her public grief as strange or excessive. Garrett contacted her immediately after Ryan was found dead, hoping to understand the final months, but she repeatedly said she was busy and never followed through with a call; Garrett eventually blocked her.

This is a legitimate post-death observation but weak evidence of pre-death motive. A benign reading is that Nienke was devastated, had access to the dance community, and lacked a socially recognized grief category for an intense but non-romantic relationship. A less charitable reading is that she was willing to occupy a public grieving role while avoiding private accountability or difficult family conversations. The record does not allow a firm conclusion.

The strongest fair statement is: Nienke's post-death behavior appeared emotionally incongruent to some people, especially the combination of public memorial prominence and private non-engagement with Garrett. Motive unknown.

## Behavioral Definitions for the Case File

**Interpersonal hypervigilance:** A heightened tendency to scan social interactions for hidden motives, manipulation, pathology, or danger. Ryan explicitly wondered whether he had become hypersensitive to traits he "couldn't unsee."

**Pathologizing:** Explaining conflict primarily through diagnostic or quasi-diagnostic labels applied to the other person, such as narcissism, borderline traits, manipulation, toxicity, or personality pathology. In this file, "pathologizing" is descriptive, not a claim that Ryan was always wrong.

**Externalization:** Organizing conflict around what the other person did wrong while giving less attention to one's own role. The boxes example is the clearest: Ryan knew Garrett had asked for the boxes and offered shipping, but later framed the issue as being used for storage.

**Avoidance rather than repair:** Managing conflict by blocking, withdrawing, delaying, declining, deleting, or letting the relationship die rather than directly apologizing, clarifying, negotiating, or repairing.

**Selective self-presentation:** Giving different audiences different versions of the same reality, often through emphasis or omission. Examples include the RAV4 financing story, the Rob/Indian-girl contrast, and the more suggestive Olivia version of the marathon hotel logistics.

**Rumination disguised as analysis:** Repeatedly revisiting interpersonal uncertainty with different people without reaching resolution. Ryan's repeated attempts to interpret Nienke's behavior fit this pattern.

**Interpretation-seeking:** Taking ambiguous relationship material to friends or Briana for meaning-making. With Nienke, this often produced reassurance that the romantic reading was plausible.

**Caretaking as intimacy:** Building closeness by being useful, practical, and supportive. Ryan's transportation and logistics role with Nienke is a central example.

**Ambiguous attachment:** A relationship that is emotionally and behaviorally intense but lacks a mutually agreed romantic definition. Nienke appears to have occupied this category for Ryan.

**Romantic absorption:** A tendency to let a new romantic or quasi-romantic relationship dominate time and attention. Ryan's historical tendency to vanish with girlfriends and his late-2024 Nienke focus both fit this description, though the final January withdrawal was broader.

**Partner-centered accommodation:** A pattern in which Ryan appeared to accept or adapt to a girlfriend's relational terms, family structure, social world, or constraints even when those terms limited his own preferences or independent friendships. Lauren and Cara are the clearest historical examples; Nienke may represent a later ambiguous version of the same tendency.

**Relationship-centered social migration:** A related pattern in which Ryan's active social life appeared to move into the girlfriend's or desired partner's network. Cara's friends and family vacations are the clearest example; the dance world and Nienke's Pittsburgh life may represent a less formal version.

**Functional constriction:** A depressive narrowing of life: less exercise, fewer hobbies, less dance, more sleep, less routine, fewer responses, and declining invitations. This is strongly documented beginning in late October.

**Selective withdrawal:** Avoiding some forms of contact while still accepting others. Ryan's refusal to let Nienke in while still going to Joe and Lucas's house or maintaining some bowling contact suggests that the final withdrawal was not simply total isolation; it varied by emotional demand, context, and relationship meaning.

**Minimization:** Reducing alarming internal or functional deterioration to less concerning explanations such as being tired, busy, rundown, or physically unwell. Ryan's February replies to Brad and John are key examples.

**Preparatory behavior:** Concrete action that may indicate movement from ideation or despair toward suicide planning. The late-January rope purchase is the most significant known example, but exact intent cannot be proven from purchase alone.

**Future-oriented behavior amid danger:** Ordinary practical planning that continues while suicide risk may still be present. Ryan's bowling-fee payment, calculator math, and dinner plans fit this category. Such behavior complicates retrospective interpretation but does not rule out suicide preparation.

**Counter-accusation instead of repair:** Responding to another person's hurt or request for acknowledgment by pointing to an alleged equivalent fault in that person. The 2022 Facebook relationship-update conflict is the clearest historical example.

## Final Case Narrative

Ryan's final year appears to have been marked by a sharp contrast between outward social momentum and inward instability. Some of the relational patterns visible in that final year, however, appear to have older roots.

Around 2015, Ryan reportedly maintained a years-long relationship with Lauren despite a major religious/sexual mismatch: Ryan was essentially atheist or nonreligious, while Lauren was strongly Christian and sexually conservative. The relationship reportedly did not include intercourse, though Ryan described it as involving "everything but that." Ryan visited her at her parents' home on weekends, slept on the downstairs couch, and complained for years that her thinking was illogical. When Garrett asked why he stayed, Ryan's answer was simple: he liked her. Later, with Cara, Ryan reportedly shifted heavily into her social and family world, spending time with her friends and joining her family's summer vacations. These histories suggest that Ryan could allow his own social world, sexual preferences, or frustrations to become secondary to the structure of a relationship with a woman he wanted to remain close to.

By 2023, there were already signs of a particular interpersonal style: Ryan was increasingly fluent in psychological explanations for other people's behavior. After Cara ended the relationship, he reportedly relayed that his therapist thought Cara might have borderline pathology. Later, Garrett and Heather noticed Ryan describing ordinary conflict through manipulation and narcissism. This did not begin with Nienke. By the time Sarah, Mallory, Sasha, and others entered the 2024 record, Ryan already had a framework for sorting people into psychologically suspect categories.

At the same time, Ryan's own accounts were not always complete. The RAV4 story, the boxes, and later the Nienke/hotel retelling show a pattern in which Ryan could give different audiences different versions of reality. Sometimes the differences were omissions. Sometimes they preserved his self-image. Sometimes they emphasized the romantic or morally favorable version of events. This matters because much of the record consists of Ryan narrating other people to third parties. His narration is evidence, but it is not neutral evidence.

The family/home context gives one possible backdrop. Ryan's house, as described, was a house of chronic unfinishedness: missing patio, dangerous door/drop, plywood floors, unfinished trim, and basic facilities left unresolved. That history should not be overused. It does not diagnose anyone and does not explain suicide. But it is relevant to a life pattern in which major unresolved situations could remain in place far longer than outsiders would expect.

Spring 2024 then introduces clinical complexity. Ryan was not simply a socially awkward man who later became sad over a woman. He had mood swings, Lexapro involvement, psychiatrist contact, severe activation, and then a later elevated period discussed with Briana in possible hypomania language. Whether this reflected medication effect, underlying mood disorder, anxiety, depression, situational stress, or some combination is unknown. What is established is that Ryan and Briana recognized mood instability well before the final months.

Summer 2024 brought the high point: women, dance, fitness, confidence, attention, and self-esteem reinforcement. Ryan noticed the mechanism himself. He said he rarely received that much attention, described it as a positive feedback loop, and explicitly linked self-confidence, self-esteem, self-worth, and mood. That statement is one of the most important in the file because it connects social/romantic reinforcement to mood without requiring retrospective speculation.

Nienke arrived inside that reward system. By July and August, she was not merely another dance acquaintance. The record shows repeated outings, IKEA, lunch, prom, outdoor trips, gym, baseball, football, ice cream, and later travel. By September, the relationship had become intense enough that Ryan was narrating it to multiple friends. Nienke had a boyfriend, but Ryan understood the relationship as open without knowing the rules. He heard her complain about Maarten. He believed Maarten did not support her in ways Ryan did. He heard that Maarten would not dance, while Ryan knew dancing was something Nienke loved and found romantic. In light of the Lauren and Cara histories, Ryan's willingness to accept a partner-defined relationship structure, even one that constrained or displaced him, was not new.

Ryan did not simply imagine all ambiguity. He reported physical closeness: holding, wanting to be held, head on shoulder, touch, and behavior that would make observers think they were sexually involved. Olivia and Mackenzie reinforced his romantic reading. One friend offered the benign "European" explanation, but even that shows Ryan was asking others to decode the relationship. He was not confident; he was trying to understand what category Nienke belonged in.

Olivia's later account intensifies this reading while remaining partly interpretive. She described Ryan as completely obsessed with Nienke, said the relationship looked like dating at baseline, suspected that Ryan and Nienke had been hooking up, and believed Nienke acted protective or territorial around him when other women from salsa/dance were involved. Olivia also reported that when Ryan returned her Christmas present in January, he was still discussing Nienke and remained defensive when Olivia said she disliked Nienke and found the situation suspicious. That account supports the possibility that Ryan still had hope regarding Nienke into January, although the exact nature of the relationship remained unresolved.

The key contradiction is Mallory. Ryan saw Mallory's desire to have something in place before leaving a long relationship as a serious red flag, and he accepted Briana's reported interpretation that Mallory was controlling the situation. Yet Ryan was simultaneously in a not entirely dissimilar emotional position with Nienke: waiting in the wings while a partnered woman gave him attention and discussed problems with her boyfriend. Ryan could identify the pattern from the outside but not fully metabolize it when he was inside it.

The Maarten triangle deepened that contradiction. Ryan met Maarten, assessed him, joked about bailing him out, heard about arrests, watched Nienke react, and then told Mackenzie he was waiting to hear whether he would be excommunicated or whether they would break up. That is not passive friendship. It is a conscious position beside an existing relationship. The missing piece is that no one in the recovered discussions appears to ask what this was like for Maarten, or whether Ryan's presence was itself part of the conflict.

The marathon weekend likely mattered because it concentrated the ambiguity. Ryan drove Nienke to a major personal event while Maarten stayed behind. They had lodging logistics, time away, dancing by water, family introduction, birthday context, and Ryan wondering beforehand whether something might happen. Afterward there was "no canoodling," but there was also a deeply warm card saying Ryan made Pittsburgh 1000x better. Ryan sent the card to Mackenzie, which suggests he did not simply experience it as rejection. The likely result was not closure, but continued ambiguity: enough warmth to preserve attachment, not enough romantic movement to resolve it.

Late October prevents a simple causal story. Ryan was still seeing Nienke after the marathon. They went to Kinzua on October 20 and Trace on October 26. Then on October 27 he told Briana about a broad depressive shift: down mood, sleep increase, less energy, difficulty with hobbies, exercise, and routines. Briana recognized seasonal depression. The next day Ryan told Olivia that his mood, motivation, and energy were sinking, that he might leave dance, that lifting was inconsistent, and that he was using a light box. This is the point where the case file must pivot from "what happened with Nienke?" to "what was happening to Ryan?"

Through November and December, Nienke's life expanded while Ryan's contracted. Her parents came, she went to NYC, then the Southwest, then Europe, then Hawaii. Ryan's communication declined sharply. But the January initiation data shows the decline was not simply her abandoning him. By January, she initiated all active conversation days and Ryan initiated none. He no longer served as the constant driver/helper, but she continued to pursue him. That is important because it complicates the painful but too-simple story that she used him for rides and discarded him.

Still, the late-October-through-holiday WhatsApp pattern may have felt asymmetrical before January. Nienke appears at times to have left town without saying goodbye, later contacts sometimes centered on pickup or ride needs, and Ryan's Christmas and New Year's messages were not promptly answered or reciprocated. The careful formulation is not "exploitation proven," but "asymmetry felt": the relationship could have mattered to both people while still becoming painful for Ryan because ordinary continuity, acknowledgment, and practical support did not appear balanced.

At the same time, Ryan was presenting other romantic stories to other people. He told Rob at Christmas about Sreyashi Mukherjee, an Indian woman he was dating, and apparently did not mention Nienke. He told Nienke about Beth kissing him in unusually detailed terms. He dated, or tried to date, while the recovered photo archive remained dominated by Nienke. This suggests that conventional dating and primary emotional salience were not the same thing.

January is where the tone of the record changes. On January 10, Ryan appears to have deleted all messages. He also began declining invitations as rundown or ill. He told Brad he was being a hermit and that it was both physical and mental. By late January, Nienke confronted him: his ignoring her was hurtful, that was not how friends communicate, and if he no longer wanted to be friends he should say so. Ryan answered that he was exceptionally down and needed time alone, and said she had done nothing wrong.

Yet Ryan's withdrawal was not absolute. When Nienke came to his apartment, he reportedly did not let her in and appeared to pretend he was sleeping, while still going to Joe and Lucas's house and remaining intermittently connected to bowling. This suggests that Nienke may have become a high-emotional-demand contact Ryan avoided, not merely one person among many he lacked energy to see.

Around that same period, he purchased the rope later associated with his death. The item was a half-inch utility rope, Ryan was not known to be a climber, and it was reportedly his only Amazon order from 2025. Approximately two weeks later, during Valentine's Day weekend, Ryan died by hanging using that rope.

That is the strongest concrete warning marker in the recovered evidence. It does not mean Nienke's confrontation caused the purchase. The content of the exchange actually cuts against a clean rejection story: Nienke was reaching for him, not ending the relationship. Ryan reassured her she had done nothing wrong. The more defensible interpretation is that by late January, Ryan was no longer merely depressed and withdrawn; he may have begun preparing.

February then shows the painful limit of ordinary friendship and neighborly care. Olivia, Nienke, Brad, Aaron, John, Charlie, Joe, Lucas, and others were trying to get through or noticing deterioration. They offered dinner, coffee, walks, football, bowling, visits, food, company, and hugs. Brad and John explicitly challenged Ryan's reassurance. Joe and Lucas reportedly cooked extra food because they wondered whether Ryan was eating properly, and they noticed signs that he was not taking care of himself. Ryan also gave friends money for the upcoming bowling competition season, had calculator math on his phone showing the fee allocation, and appears to have had general dinner plans for the following week. He also secretly returned Olivia's Christmas present by leaving it at her door. These facts sit uneasily beside one another: ordinary future planning, possible emotional leave-taking, and concrete suicide preparation appear to overlap. Ryan minimized: tired, busy, all good. In hindsight, those responses look like concealment. In real time, they may have sounded like the kind of thing a depressed friend says when he wants space.

This is one of the central conclusions of the case file: Ryan was not forgotten. The record shows multiple people noticing, trying, and escalating within ordinary social limits. What they did not have was the full cross-sectional picture: Briana had the therapy side, Nienke had her relationship, Olivia had the romantic interpretation and later invitations, Brad and John had the withdrawal, Joe and Lucas had daily-life/self-care observations, Charlie had unanswered calls, the browser history had death/consciousness searches, Amazon had the rope timeline, and the photo archive had the intensity of Nienke's presence. No one person had all of it while Ryan was alive.

The final record therefore resists blame-shaped simplicity. Nienke mattered. Briana mattered. Lexapro and psychiatric care may have mattered, but the evidence is incomplete. Family dysfunction may have shaped Ryan's patterns, but it does not explain the final act. Ryan's own self-presentation made him harder to read. His friends tried, but he minimized. His romantic hopes around Nienke were real, but his depression was broader than Nienke. The rope purchase is concrete, but its trigger is unknown.

The most accurate summary is that Ryan's death followed a narrowing process. His world had been wide in summer: dance, women, fitness, attention, confidence, road trips, friendship, and possibility. By winter it had constricted: fatigue, sleep, less dance, less lifting, less routine, fewer replies, message deletion, minimized reassurances, and finally concrete preparation. The people around him were still reaching in. Ryan was increasingly unable or unwilling to reach back.

## Three Columbo-Style Case Theories

These theories are not conclusions and are not mutually exclusive. Each is a way of arranging the known facts into a possible explanation for why Ryan died by suicide. The strongest reading of the file may be that all three were operating at once.

### Theory 1: The Depressive Constriction Theory

The core theory is that Ryan's death was primarily the end stage of a depressive episode that began no later than late October 2024 and progressively narrowed his life.

This theory explains the broadest range of evidence: hypersomnia, loss of energy, reduced exercise, leaving or considering leaving dance, diminished hobbies/routines, brain fog, work drain, inconsistent lifting, reduced communication, January social withdrawal, self-care decline observed by Joe and Lucas, friends becoming worried, and Ryan minimizing his state as tired or busy. It also explains why the withdrawal was not Nienke-specific. By late January and February, Ryan was pulling away from nearly everyone.

The "one more thing" in this theory is that Ryan recognized the downturn early. He told Briana, discussed seasonal depression, asked about Lexapro, bought a light box, and told Olivia his mood, motivation, and energy were sinking. This was not invisible to Ryan. The mystery is how a downturn he could identify in October became a suicide by February.

What this theory does not fully explain is the timing of the rope purchase around the Nienke confrontation, the emotional meaning of returning Olivia's gift, the Valentine's Day weekend timing, or why Ryan continued ordinary future-oriented behavior such as bowling payments and dinner plans. Those facts do not defeat the theory; they suggest the depressive process may have coexisted with ambivalence, concealment, habit, or compartmentalization.

### Theory 2: The Nienke / Self-Worth Collapse Theory

The core theory is that Ryan's depressive episode became more dangerous because the Nienke relationship had become a major self-worth scaffold during a period when Ryan was unusually dependent on attention from women for confidence, mood, and identity.

This theory explains why Nienke dominates the photographic record, why Ryan discussed her with multiple confidantes, why he did not pathologize her the way he pathologized other women, why he tolerated the Maarten triangle, and why the marathon weekend mattered. It also explains the emotional force of the late-January confrontation: Nienke was not rejecting him romantically, but she was telling him that he was failing as a friend. For someone whose bond with her was built partly around being useful, attentive, supportive, and special, becoming the "bad friend" may have struck a particularly painful identity wound.

The theory also explains why late-October-through-holiday asymmetry may matter: unanswered Christmas/New Year's messages, leaving town without goodbye, and practical pickup/ride-oriented contact could have felt to Ryan like a demotion from special companion to useful support person.

The "one more thing" in this theory is the timing: Nienke confronts him around January 25-26; Ryan says he is exceptionally down and needs isolation; the rope is purchased around the same period; the death occurs roughly two weeks later, on Valentine's Day weekend. That does not prove causation. It does make the Nienke rupture or confrontation difficult to dismiss as irrelevant.

This theory is strengthened by the selectivity of Ryan's avoidance. He reportedly would not let Nienke into his apartment and appeared to pretend he was sleeping, even while he still went to Joe and Lucas's house and remained connected enough to bowling to pay fees and calculate costs. That does not mean he was well. It means Nienke may have been uniquely difficult for him to face.

Olivia's later theory fits here: she believed Ryan was probably waiting for Nienke, thought the timing just after Valentine's Day was not coincidental, and imagined that some form of ultimatum may have occurred and gone badly. That is a coherent theory, but it remains unproven. The recovered record does not currently establish an ultimatum.

What this theory does not fully explain is that Ryan's depressive downturn was already documented on October 27, while he was still seeing Nienke, and that January-February withdrawal was broader than her alone. Nienke also pursued him when he withdrew, offered contact, and told him isolation would not help. Therefore, this is not a theory that Nienke caused the suicide. It is a theory that Ryan's attachment to her may have been one emotionally important stressor inside an already dangerous depressive process.

### Theory 3: The Shame / Self-Image Collapse Theory

The core theory is that Ryan's suicide risk increased when the gap between his preferred self-image and his actual functioning became too large to manage.

Ryan often occupied the role of competent helper, driver, advisor, analyst, emotionally perceptive friend, desirable man, and independent adult. But the record also shows contradictory material: parental financial support minimized to others, the RAV4 story, the boxes, selective self-presentation, difficulty apologizing or repairing, erectile-dysfunction distress, job dissatisfaction, romantic dependency, declining hygiene/self-care, possible poor eating, and increasing reliance on others while telling them he was fine.

The "one more thing" in this theory is that Ryan was skilled at identifying manipulation, pathology, and inconsistency in other people, but less able to metabolize his own contradictions. By late January, he was no longer only the helper. He was the person being chased, worried about, fed, checked on, confronted, and possibly seen as failing. Returning Olivia's Christmas present may fit this frame: not merely sadness, but shame, unworthiness, or an attempt to remove a relational debt.

This theory explains the January 10 message deletion, the absence of recovered apology language, the defensive counter-accusation pattern from 2022, the different stories told to different audiences, and the late-stage minimization to Brad and John. It also fits the family/home theme of chronic unfinishedness: unresolved things could remain unresolved until they became part of the structure.

What this theory does not fully explain is why shame would culminate in suicide at that specific time rather than earlier, or why Ryan still engaged in practical future planning around bowling and dinner. It also risks over-interpreting character patterns as motive. The safer conclusion is narrower: shame and self-image conflict may have made it harder for Ryan to disclose the true severity of his condition or accept help once he was deteriorating.
